MD2PDF

A Markdown-to-PDF editor that runs entirely in your browser. You type, it renders a live A4 preview, and you export a clean PDF. Nothing is uploaded, so your document never leaves your machine.

The problem

I kept hitting the same loop: write something in Markdown, then bounce over to a heavy third-party converter or an AI tool just to get a clean PDF. It worked, but every time it meant extra steps, and pasting my documents into someone else's service.

I wanted the opposite: type, see exactly what the page will look like, export. No uploads, no waiting, no account.

The approach

  • Parse and sanitize: Markdown goes through Marked with GitHub-flavored Markdown turned on, and the resulting HTML is cleaned with DOMPurify before it's shown, so pasted content can't inject scripts.
  • Lay it out as real pages: the preview is drawn on A4-sized pages (794 × 1122 px at 96 DPI). A pagination pass moves any block that would straddle a page boundary onto the next page, and the preview shows the page breaks and page count as you type.
  • Export what you see: the same HTML is rendered off-screen at A4 width, captured at 2× resolution with html2canvas, cut into page-sized slices, and assembled into a PDF with jsPDF.
  • Make it pleasant to write in: a formatting toolbar with keyboard shortcuts, custom text colors with quick presets, synchronized scrolling, three layouts (Split, Focus editor, Preview), dark and light themes, .md drag-and-drop, and a short first-run guide.

Architecture

A React 19 + TypeScript single-page app built with Vite and styled with Tailwind CSS. There is no backend: it's static files on Vercel, and all the work happens in the browser.

Key decisions

Nothing leaves the browser

Privacy was the reason to build it, so parsing, preview and PDF generation all run client-side. The app code makes no network requests with your content, and there's no server to run or pay for.

One paginator for preview and PDF

The preview and the exporter call the same page-break function, so the pages you see while typing are the pages you get in the file. No surprises after you click export.

Keep headings with their content

A heading looks ahead at the start of the next block, so it never ends up alone at the bottom of a page. Lists taller than a page break between items instead of through the middle of one.

Sanitize, but allow what documents need

DOMPurify strips anything dangerous, with a small allowlist for what real documents use: details/summary, kbd, mark, and inline styles for custom text colors.

Scroll sync without feedback loops

Editor and preview scroll by proportion. Whichever pane you're scrolling takes the lead for a moment, so the two panes don't keep nudging each other back and forth.

Fidelity first

Capturing the rendered page as an image makes the PDF match the preview exactly, including colors and layout. The tradeoff is that PDF text isn't selectable yet, which is the natural next improvement.
